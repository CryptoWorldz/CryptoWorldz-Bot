# OneWorldz Platform Power Audit

Status: design-time audit framework

## Purpose
Audit the technology platforms OneWorldz uses with the same public-interest standards OneWorldz intends to discuss on its Laws and Learn resources.

This is not a claim that any named company has broken a law. Findings must separate verified facts, design risks, opinions, and proposed safeguards.

## Audit lenses
1. User control — Can people export, delete, leave, appeal, and choose alternatives?
2. Transparency — Are pricing, limits, automated decisions, moderation, data use, and material restrictions understandable before commitment?
3. Lock-in — Does the architecture create avoidable dependency on one vendor, proprietary format, identity provider, hosting layer, or AI model?
4. Data and privacy — What is collected, retained, shared, inferred, or required?
5. Economic power — Can fees, subscriptions, platform rules, ranking, access, or distribution materially disadvantage users or small builders?
6. Automation accountability — Can AI-generated or automated actions be inspected, corrected, attributed, and overridden by a human?
7. Reliability — Can a platform report success while the user-facing outcome is broken? Verification must test the actual outcome.
8. Interoperability — Are open formats, APIs, portable assets, standard domains, and replaceable components used where practical?
9. Accessibility and inclusion — Can ordinary users understand and operate the system without unnecessary technical barriers?
10. Remedy — Is there a documented complaint, correction, refund, appeal, rollback, or escalation route?

## OneWorldz build rules derived from the audit
- Own the domains and keep deployable source/assets portable.
- Preserve approved original media independently of generated replacements.
- Do not treat a successful build/deployment status as proof of a successful website.
- Verify live pages, links, images, mobile rendering, and critical actions after deployment.
- Keep backups and rollback artifacts before production changes.
- Never invent token contracts, partnerships, endorsements, legal conclusions, or live trading status.
- Label proposals, tests, simulations, and live production functions distinctly.
- Keep human override for consequential automation.
- Document third-party dependencies and an exit/replacement path for each critical dependency.

## LearnWorldz reference format
Each platform/resource entry should show:
- What it does
- Why OneWorldz uses it
- What power/dependency it creates
- What data or permissions are involved
- What the user can control
- Known limitations
- OneWorldz mitigation/exit plan
- Evidence links and date checked

## Initial audit subjects
- AI assistants and model/API providers
- Source control and CI/CD
- Hosting and domain infrastructure
- Databases/authentication
- Payment processors
- Social platforms
- Token launch/trading/indexing services
- Fundraising platforms

## Publication standard
Public audit pages should use evidence-first language. Criticism may be strong, but factual claims need sources and dates. The page should distinguish current platform behavior from OneWorldz policy proposals and fictional/creative material such as the 2030 Jesus Returns project.
