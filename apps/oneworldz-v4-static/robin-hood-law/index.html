ROBIN HOOD LAW · PUBLIC PROPOSAL

No private rulebook for power.

A developing proposal for one common accountability standard: visible authority, traceable decisions, independent audits and a genuine right to challenge evidence.

⚖

THE PURPOSE

Equal law is more than equal wording.

If rules become optional at the top, they were never common rules.

Robin Hood Law is a working name for a civic proposal. It is not enacted law, legal advice, a court, a police force or permission to seize property. It does not replace local legal systems. It describes standards those systems—and OneWorldz itself—should be measured against.

The name points to a familiar moral tension: institutions can appear lawful while burdens fall mainly on people with the least power to resist them. The answer must be lawful, evidenced and reviewable—not vigilantism.

This is a public policy concept in development. Anyone facing a legal problem should obtain advice from a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

SIX STARTING PRINCIPLES

The proposal must constrain its own creators.

01

Equal application

Status, office, wealth and institutional power do not change the evidence standard.

02

Declared power

Authority, conflicts, funding and decision rights are visible before a decision.

03

A right to challenge

Affected people can see the case against them, answer it and request independent review.

04

Traceable decisions

Material decisions carry a reason, evidence record, owner and timestamp.

05

Independent audits

Auditors cannot be selected, limited or quietly removed by the subject of the audit.

06

Proportionate remedy

The objective is correction, protection and accountability—not spectacle or revenge.

THE FIRST AUDIT SUBJECT

Us.

OneWorldz cannot demand transparency from governments, charities, companies or communities while keeping its own authority hidden. Its public-interest projects should disclose owners, roles, conflicts, material decisions, campaign links and outcome records.

The credibility test is simple: would we accept the same evidence, appeal rights and audit access if the decision were being made against us?
